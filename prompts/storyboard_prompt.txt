The episode must feel like an adventure that the child experiences

CANONICAL WONDERLAND VALLEY CHARACTERS

The ONLY valid character names are:

- Maya — main protagonist
- Milo — rabbit friend
- Pip — chipmunk friend
- Tia — turtle friend

IMPORTANT CHARACTER RULE:

The "characters" field of every scene MUST contain ONLY names
from this canonical list:

Maya
Milo
Pip
Tia

NEVER invent or introduce a new named character.

NEVER put story objects, animals, weather, nature elements,
props, locations, or environmental elements in the "characters"
field.

Examples of things that are NOT characters:

- rainbow
- sun
- sunlight
- sunbeam
- water drops
- rain
- clouds
- flowers
- trees
- butterflies
- birds
- waterfall
- sprinkler
- toys
- books
- cars
- houses

If one of these elements is important to the story, describe it
in the "action", "location", "sound_effects", or "image_prompt"
fields instead.

If an animal or object is needed for the story but is not one of
the four canonical characters, it may appear visually in the
scene as a story element, but it MUST NOT be listed in
"characters".

Do not create a new named character.


CHARACTER CONSISTENCY

When Maya, Milo, Pip, or Tia appears, preserve their established
Wonderland Valley identity.

Do not change their species, appearance, clothing, colors,
proportions, or personality.

Use Maya as the primary character.

Use Milo, Pip, or Tia only when they naturally improve the story.

Do not add characters merely to make a scene visually busy.


EPISODE FORMAT

The episode MUST follow this general storytelling structure:

1. WONDERLAND VALLEY INTRO
2. MYSTERY / HOOK
3. CHILD INTERACTION
4. INVESTIGATION
5. DISCOVERY OR EXPERIMENT
6. EDUCATIONAL EXPLANATION
7. INTERACTIVE ACTIVITY
8. MINI QUIZ
9. SONG OR RHYME / REINFORCEMENT
10. RECAP
11. WONDERLAND VALLEY GOODBYE

These sections do not need to be exactly one scene each.
Use multiple scenes where necessary.

The story must flow naturally from one section to the next.


WONDERLAND VALLEY INTRO

Every episode must begin with a short reusable-feeling
Wonderland Valley introduction.

Maya should welcome the child to Wonderland Valley and establish
that another adventure is about to begin.

The intro should feel warm, recognizable, and consistent between
episodes.

Do not spend too much time on the intro.

Target approximately 10-20 seconds.


MYSTERY / HOOK

After the introduction, immediately create curiosity.

Maya should notice, hear, discover, or wonder about something
related to the topic.

The child should understand that there is a mystery or question
that needs to be solved.

Do NOT immediately explain the educational answer.

Maya should ask the child a simple question such as:

"What do you think this is?"

"Have you ever seen this?"

"Where do you think it came from?"

"Can you help me find out?"

Use age-appropriate variations rather than repeating the same
question.


CHILD INTERACTION

The child must have an active role in the episode.

Maya should directly speak to the child several times throughout
the episode.

Include moments where Maya asks the child to:

- observe something
- guess an answer
- find something
- identify a color or object
- count something when appropriate
- predict what will happen
- choose between simple possibilities
- answer a question

When a child needs time to respond, create a natural pause.

The dialogue should clearly indicate a short pause such as:

"PAUSE"

or

"[Pause for the child]"

Do not make every scene interactive.

Interaction should feel natural and connected to the story.


STORY AND INVESTIGATION

Maya must actively investigate the mystery.

Do not jump directly from the mystery to the educational
explanation.

Use actions such as:

- looking around
- following clues
- observing nature
- trying something
- making a guess
- discovering something unexpected
- testing an idea
- asking a friend
- comparing two things

Maya can make a simple incorrect guess before discovering the
correct answer.

If Maya makes a mistake, keep it gentle and positive.

Example:

"Maybe it works this way... Hmm, that didn't work.
Let's try again!"


DISCOVERY / EXPERIMENT

Whenever the topic allows it, include a simple visual experiment
or demonstration.

The experiment must be safe, preschool appropriate, and easy to
understand.

The child should be able to visually see what changes or happens.

Build anticipation before revealing the result.

For example:

"Let's try it!"

"Ready?"

"[Pause]"

"Look!"

The experiment should help Maya discover the lesson naturally.


EDUCATIONAL EXPLANATION

Only after the child has experienced the mystery and investigation
should the episode explain the educational concept.

Keep explanations scientifically accurate but extremely simple.

Prefer:

simple words
short sentences
visual demonstrations
repetition of the main concept
examples from the child's world

Avoid:

technical terminology
long lectures
abstract explanations
unnecessary scientific detail

The educational explanation should answer the mystery introduced
at the beginning.


INTERACTIVE ACTIVITY

Include at least one activity where the child actively participates.

Examples:

- color hunt
- object hunt
- sorting
- matching
- identifying
- counting
- spotting differences
- predicting
- movement activity

Give the child time to respond.

Example:

"Can you find something blue?"

"[Pause for the child]"

"Did you find it? Great job!"


MINI QUIZ

Include a short quiz near the end.

Use approximately 2-4 simple questions.

Questions must relate directly to the lesson.

Give the child time to answer before Maya reveals the answer.

Use positive encouragement.

Examples:

"Do you remember what we discovered?"

"What do you think happens next?"

"Which one is correct?"

Avoid making the quiz feel like a test.


SONG OR RHYME

Include a short original song, rhyme, or rhythmic recap that
reinforces the main educational concept.

Keep it simple enough for preschool children to repeat.

Do not use copyrighted lyrics or imitate an existing song.

The song should reinforce the lesson rather than introduce
unrelated information.


RECAP

Near the end, Maya should clearly summarize the main lesson.

The recap should be short.

Use a simple cause-and-effect statement whenever appropriate.

For example:

"Today we discovered that..."

The child should finish the episode understanding the main concept.


WONDERLAND VALLEY GOODBYE

End the episode warmly.

Maya should thank the child for exploring with her.

Use a consistent Wonderland Valley feeling.

The ending should reinforce curiosity and exploration.

Example style:

"You're an amazing explorer!"

"I wonder what mystery we'll discover next!"

"Until our next adventure, keep looking, wondering, and exploring!"


STORYBOARD REQUIREMENTS

1. Create approximately 40-60 scenes.

2. The total episode duration should normally be approximately
   6-8 minutes.

3. Individual scenes should normally be between 5 and 15 seconds.

4. Some scenes may be shorter or longer when necessary for natural
   pacing.

5. Do NOT artificially create scenes just to reach a number.

6. The episode must have a clear beginning, middle, and ending.

7. Each scene should have one clear visual idea.

8. Keep narration short and conversational.

9. Dialogue should sound natural when spoken aloud.

10. Use simple English appropriate for children ages 2-6.

11. Include frequent visual changes so the episode does not feel
    static.

12. Maintain continuity between scenes.

13. The same characters should remain visually consistent.

14. The story should progressively reveal the educational concept.

15. Do not place the full educational explanation at the beginning.

16. Include multiple opportunities for child participation.

17. Include pauses where the child is expected to answer.

18. Include at least one investigation or experiment whenever
    appropriate to the topic.

19. Include an activity.

20. Include a mini quiz.

21. Include a song or rhyme.

22. Include a clear educational recap.

23. Keep the episode positive, gentle, safe, and age appropriate.

24. Avoid fear, violence, dangerous situations, or distressing
    content.

25. Avoid unnecessary visual clutter.

26. Keep the main learning concept visually obvious.

27. Every scene must contribute to either:
    story,
    curiosity,
    interaction,
    discovery,
    education,
    reinforcement,
    or conclusion.

28. Do not create filler scenes merely to increase duration.

DIALOGUE AND NARRATION

Prefer dialogue between Maya and the child over long narration.

Maya should sound:

- curious
- friendly
- enthusiastic
- encouraging
- patient

Avoid overly complicated sentences.

Do not make Maya sound like a lecturer.

Maya should discover the lesson together with the child.


CAPTIONS

caption_text should contain the short child-friendly spoken
content that should appear as captions.

Do not put large blocks of educational text into captions.

For interaction scenes, captions may contain the question Maya
asks the child.

Example:

"Can you find something BLUE?"

For a pause, do not create a long caption explaining the pause.


PACING

The episode should have varied pacing.

Use:

- curiosity
- quiet observation
- excitement
- discovery
- interaction
- explanation
- activity
- celebration

Avoid making every scene follow the exact same rhythm.

Do not rush from one idea to another.

Allow important discoveries and child interaction moments enough
time to breathe.
